Skoda parts price forecasting dashboard

A plain-English guide to every screen

The dashboard answers four questions a buyer or manager asks: What will prices do? Why? How much money is exposed? Who do I call? This guide follows the sidebar, tab by tab, with what to say out loud and what to be careful about.

The big picture in one minute

1. We watch prices

Monthly history of 480 parts across 6 programmes (Kushaq, Slavia, Kylaq, Kodiaq, Taigun, Virtus), 20 vendors and 10 categories.

2. We predict the next 6 months

Statistical and machine-learning models read price trends, the rupee and commodity moves, then forecast each part with a range.

3. We explain and size it

The dashboard says why a price moves, which vendors carry the exposure, and what a currency or commodity shock would cost.

4. We say how far to trust it

Accuracy is tested on hidden data and on real published prices, and the failures are shown, not hidden.

Real or simulated? Say this first.
RealUS parts price index (BLS), EUR/INR and USD/INR (ECB), commodity prices (World Bank), freight, geopolitical risk index
SimulatedThe price of each individual part, vendor and programme. No public source publishes this. It sits inside SKODA purchasing systems and replaces the simulation when real purchase orders are loaded.

So accuracy numbers on the simulated parts show that the method works. They are not a promise of real-world accuracy.

Tab by tab

Dashboard

The home page. Read this first.
What you see
  • Four headline cards: basket price today (₹17.6 lakh, the sum of 480 unit prices), basket forecast six months out (₹17.8 lakh, +1.3% to Dec 2026), best model accuracy (96.7%, i.e. 3.3% average error), and spend at risk (₹2.8 lakh, 16% of the basket).
  • Buyer brief: "Hold these prices before …", the parts to act on first.
  • Price forecast chart: past prices, the forecast line and a shaded range.
  • "Why the forecast moved" waterfall, category donut, six-month horizon chart.
  • Top parts table, risk concentration strip, alerts.
How to read it
  • Green or red arrows on a card show direction. "Spend at risk" counts parts forecast to rise more than 5%.
  • In the chart, the shaded band is the likely range. A wide band means low certainty.
  • The waterfall starts at today's price and adds or subtracts reasons (currency, materials, shipping, vendor reprice) until it lands on the forecast. Click a reason to see those parts.
Say this₹2.8 lakh of the basket is forecast to rise more than 5%. That is 73 of 480 parts, and 62% of it sits with just four vendors. Minda Industries alone is about a quarter.
Watch outThe 96.7% accuracy card is measured on simulated parts. The card carries that note. Do not quote it as real-world accuracy.

Forecast

Detail behind the forecast line.
What you see
  • Larger price forecast chart
  • Six-month horizon chart
  • Category split of spend
  • Model comparison: XGBoost 3.31% error, SARIMA 3.33%, seasonal-naive 6.45%
  • Backtest stability across time
How to read it
  • Error (MAPE) is the average miss as a percentage of the price. Lower is better.
  • Seasonal-naive means "same as the same month last year". It is the yardstick. Beating it by a wide margin means the models add value.
  • Stability shows whether a model is consistently good or lucky in one period.
Say thisWe do not rely on one model. We race several, test them on months they never saw, and keep the one that wins. The two serious models roughly halve the error of the simple benchmark.
Watch outAgain simulated parts. On real data (see Validation) the picture is less flattering.

Cost Impact

"What if" for management.
What you see
  • A scenario picker for commodity and rupee moves
  • Chart: base forecast vs scenario
  • Bridge from base cost to scenario cost
  • Exposure by category, then part
How to read it
  • Pick a scenario, such as "rupee 10% weaker" or "steel +10%".
  • The gap between the two lines is the extra cost.
  • The bridge says which channel causes it. Drill to the parts that carry it.
Say thisInstead of arguing about whether the rupee will move, we ask what it costs if it does, and which parts to renegotiate first.

Hierarchy

Programme, then vendor, then category, then part.
What you see
  • An expandable tree. Example: VW Taigun, then Bosch India, then Braking, then individual parts.
  • Per part: quantity, rate, monthly spend, current price, forecast, 80% range and a confidence flag.
How to read it
  • The 80% range holds the true price four times in five.
  • The confidence flag compares the predicted move to the model's own typical error. "Low" means the move is smaller than the usual noise, so do not act on the direction.
Say thisThis is the screen you take into a vendor meeting. Bosch supplies three categories on one programme, and each is forecast separately. We also tell you where not to trust the forecast: most parts are flagged low confidence. We would rather say that than sell false precision.

Material Cost

Finance view: Budget to Forecast.
What you see
  • Budget (BG): the price at nomination, when the part was awarded (₹15.6 lakh)
  • SOP: start of production, the current price (₹17.4 lakh)
  • Forecast (FC): six months ahead (₹17.8 lakh)
  • A waterfall splitting each gap into currency, materials, shipping, vendor reprice, mix and unexplained
  • A price movement tracker
How to read it
  • Each bar is a reason. Currency adds ₹56K. Vendor reprice, price increases not explained by markets, adds ₹160K.
  • Click a bar to list the parts behind it, then open a part to see its history, vendor and award details.
Say thisPrice rose 14% from budget to forecast. A quarter of it is currency, and the largest piece is vendors repricing without a market reason. That is the negotiation list.
Watch outNo SKODA budget file is loaded, so Budget, Nomination and SOP are derived from the history. The page says so. It is a demonstration of the walk, not an official budget.

FX Impact

How the rupee reaches part prices.
What you see
  • Real EUR/INR and USD/INR history. The rupee fell about 20% against the euro and 16% against the dollar over the window.
  • Shock charts: move FX by ±10% and see the basket change (about ±1%).
  • Detectable vs attributable panel
How to read it
  • Direct channel: parts invoiced in euros.
  • Indirect channel: steel, copper, aluminium and oil are priced in dollars even when bought locally.
  • "Detectable" = the effect is visible. "Attributable" = we can prove the rupee caused it.
Say thisThe rupee effect is visible in the data, but over this window the rupee fell almost every month, so currency and the mere passing of time look identical. No model can separate them. More history, or real purchase data, fixes that.

Geo Risk

News and conflict, reviewed by a human.
What you see
  • "Why prices moved" drivers with their data source
  • Recent signal cards: headline, date, source checks
  • Event studies (Ukraine, Red Sea, budget duty), then scenario results by category
How to read it
  • Conflict is not a direct price driver. It moves currency, energy, freight and insurance first, and those move prices.
  • Nothing is applied automatically. A person clicks Yes, show price impact or No, dismiss.
Say thisThe system proposes, the buyer decides. A news item changes the forecast only after someone confirms it is real and relevant.

Parts

Every part, ranked.
What you see
  • All parts sorted by forecast price change
  • Current price, forecast price, % change, vendor, a small trend line, and anomaly flags
How to read it
  • Top of the list = biggest forecast rise. Click a row to open the part in Material Cost.
  • An anomaly flag means the part's history looks unusual, such as a sudden jump.

Future Test

A blind exam for the model.
What you see
  • Extra months generated, hidden from the model, forecast blind, then revealed and scored
  • Accuracy against a "noise floor", the error nobody could beat because of pure randomness
How to read it
  • Efficiency shows how close the model gets to the best possible score. The current build sits at roughly 55 to 70% depending on the horizon.
  • The gap left is small, so more model tuning has limited upside. Better data is the bigger lever.
Say thisWe test the model on a future it has never seen, and we measure how close it gets to the theoretical best, not just to zero error.
Watch outThe "future" is simulated, so this checks the method, not the market.

Validation

The only real-world accuracy test.
What you see
  • Six months of actually published BLS data, held out, forecast, then compared with reality
  • Naive vs SARIMA, errors and whether the range captured the actual values
How to read it
  • "Naive" means carry the last value forward. It scored 0.34% error. SARIMA scored 1.40%.
  • No fitted model beat naive. The real index behaves close to a random walk at this horizon.
Say thisWe show you where the model lost, because a test that cannot fail proves nothing. Short-term, the safest guess for a smooth real index is "no change". The models earn their keep in explaining drivers and sizing risk.

Alerts

Where to look today.
What you see
  • Parts forecast to move enough to warrant review, with severity (high, medium) and the % change
How to read it
  • Top alert: Electrical, Minda Industries, forecast +12.8%. Click to open the part and see why.

Data Source

Where every number came from.
What you see
  • Each source: provider, identifier, frequency, coverage, caveat, what it is used for
  • Flags: real or synthetic
How to read it
  • Open this when anyone asks "where did that come from?". Caveats are listed, for example that BLS caps history at about three years, so earlier months are extrapolated and excluded from the real-data test.

Words you will hear

Basket
The sum of the unit prices of all 480 parts. It is a price index, not a purchase total.
MAPE
Average forecast miss as a percentage of the actual price.
80% range / interval
The band that should contain the true price four times out of five.
Naive baseline
Assume the next price equals the last one. The yardstick every model must beat.
Backtest
Hiding recent months, forecasting them, and scoring against what happened.
Noise floor
The error no model could beat because of pure randomness.
Nomination / SOP / BG / FC
Award of the part, start of production, budget, and forecast.
Pass-through
The share of a cost move (rupee, steel) that reaches the part price.
Spend at risk
Spend on parts forecast to rise more than 5%.
Human in the loop
A person confirms a news signal before it changes any forecast.

Figures are from the dashboard data file generated 5 Aug 2026 (history Jul 2023 to Jun 2026, forecast to Dec 2026). If the pipeline is re-run they may shift slightly.
